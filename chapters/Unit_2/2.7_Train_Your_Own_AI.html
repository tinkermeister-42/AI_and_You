2.7 - Train Your Own AI

In Lesson 2.6, you trained a network on numbers. Today, you train an AI to see. It learns to recognize Rock, Paper, and Scissors hand signs, with your camera, in about 10 minutes. No code. No account.

Then you try to fool it. That’s the most important part.

Today’s Plan (40 minutes)

  • 🔥 Warm Up What should it learn? 4 min
  • 👥 Learn Together How Teachable Machine works 6 min
  • 🛠️ Try It Train it, then fool it 22 min
  • 💬 Talk About It Would it work for everyone? 8 min
  • 🏷️ label: the right answer for an example, like “rock”
  • 🏋️ training: showing an AI many examples so it can fix its weights
  • 🧪 test data: new examples the AI never trained on

Printable lesson sheet - the sheet you write on today.

🔥 Warm Up

You can train an AI to tell apart any pictures your camera can see. What would you teach it? Write two ideas on your sheet.

Examples: rock, paper, and scissors. Thumbs up and thumbs down. A pen and a pencil. A cup and a book.

👥 Learn Together

2.7.1 - How Teachable Machine Works

Teachable Machine is a free tool from Google. It runs in your web browser.

Class one kind of thing, like “rock” a label
Samples the pictures you take for a class training examples
Train Model the network fixes its weights

Preview try it on new pictures

Teachable Machine trains in your browser. Your pictures stay on your computer unless you choose to save your project to Google Drive. Do not save it.

Keep faces out of the camera. Only hands and objects should be in the pictures.

🛠️ Try It

2.7.2 - Train It

Work in a group of 2 or 3, with one computer that has a camera.

  1. Open Teachable Machine. Choose Standard image model.
  2. Rename Class 1 to “Rock” and Class 2 to “Paper.” Press Add a class and name it “Scissors.”
  3. For each class, press Webcam. Make the hand sign. Hold to Record about 50 pictures. Move your hand a little while you record.
  4. Press Train Model. Wait. Don’t switch to another tab while it trains.
  5. In Preview, make each sign. Watch the bars. Is your AI right?

2.7.3 - Fool It

Now try to make your AI wrong. Change one thing at a time. Write what happened on your sheet.

  • Use your other hand.
  • Move your hand very close to the camera, then far away.
  • Turn the lights off, or stand in front of a bright window.
  • Let your partner make the signs.

Your AI learned from your hand, in this room, in this light. When something new came in, it had never seen anything like it. So it guessed.

This is true for every AI, even very big ones. An AI is only as good as its training examples.

💬 Talk About It

Your AI worked well for you. Would it work for everyone? Who was missing from your training pictures?

  • My AI was fooled by … because …
  • My training pictures were missing …
  • A face AI trained only on people from one country might …

🚀 Go Further

Open Teachable Machine again with your partner. Train Rock, Paper, and Scissors like yesterday.

  1. Try two new tricks. Change the background: hold up a book or a poster. Then make the sign with a glove or a sock on your hand.
  2. Now make your AI harder to fool. Add more kinds of examples: both hands, different people, near and far, different light. Train again.
  3. Test all the tricks again. Did your AI get better? Write what changed on your sheet.
  4. Class model: train one model with pictures from everyone. Test it on a teacher who wasn’t in the pictures. Is it better than a one-person model?
  5. Train a new model on one of your Warm Up ideas. Then try to fool it.

Make an Audio project in Teachable Machine. Teach it to recognize “hello” in three languages that people in your class speak. Record each word from several people. Does it work for someone new?

Train an image model with three classes: paper, plastic, and metal. Use real objects from the classroom. Test it on objects it has never seen. Where does it make mistakes?
