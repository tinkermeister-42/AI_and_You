5.2 - AI Scams

Scam messages used to be easy to spot. They had bad spelling and strange grammar. “Dear costumer, you account are block.”

Not anymore. AI writes perfect messages, in any language, in seconds. It can even copy a person’s voice (Lesson 4.3). In 2025, people in the United States reported losing almost 16 billion dollars to scams.

The spelling is no longer the clue. Today you learn the clues that still work.

Today’s Plan (40 minutes)

  • 🔥 Warm Up Would you click? 5 min
  • 👥 Learn Together The warning signs 10 min
  • 🛠️ Try It Scam or real? 18 min
  • 💬 Talk About It Who do scammers target? 7 min
  • 🎣 scam: a trick to steal your money or your information
  • 🚩 warning sign: a clue that something is wrong
  • 🎭 imposter: someone who pretends to be another person or a real company
  • 🔑 verification code: a short number a website sends you to prove it’s really you

Printable lesson sheet - the sheet you write on today.

🔥 Warm Up

This text message comes to your phone. Would you click the link? Why or why not? Vote with your hand.

Delivery Service: Your package couldn’t be delivered. Update your address in 12 hours or it will be sent back: deliv-update-help.co

The spelling is perfect. But it wants you to hurry (12 hours), and it has a strange link. Were you even waiting for a package? These are warning signs.

👥 Learn Together

5.2.1 - The Warning Signs

Many scams come from an imposter: someone who pretends to be a real company, a government office, or a person you love. Almost every scam uses some of these tricks:

Warning sign What it sounds like
⏰ Hurry “Today!” “In 12 hours!” “Right now!”
😨 Fear “The police will come.” “Your account is closed.”
🎁 A prize “You won!” for something you never entered
🤫 A secret “Don’t tell anyone.” “Don’t tell Dad.”
🎴 A strange way to pay gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, payment apps
🔗 A link or a code “Click here.” “Send me the code we sent you.”

A real company or government office will never ask you to pay with gift cards. It will never ask for a verification code you got by text. When you aren’t sure, stop. Don’t click. Call the person or the company on a number you already know.

5.2.2 - Why AI Makes Scams Harder

  • Perfect language: no more spelling mistakes, in English or in your home language.
  • Fast and cheap: one scammer can send thousands of different messages.
  • Personal: AI can use things you posted online to make a message about you.
  • Fake voices: a call can sound like someone you love (Lesson 4.3). Ask for your family safe word.

So don’t look for bad spelling. Look for the warning signs.

In 2025, people in the United States reported losing $15.9 billion to scams, more than ever before (Federal Trade Commission, March 2026). Text messages were the most common way scammers first reached people. The biggest losses were paid by bank transfer and crypto.

🛠️ Try It

5.2.3 - Scam or Real?

Work with a partner. These practice messages were made up for this book.

  1. Read each message. Decide together: Scam or Real?
  2. Press your answer. Read the warning signs.
  3. On your sheet, write the warning signs you found in each scam.
  4. Which scam would be most likely to fool someone in your family? Why?

💬 Talk About It

Scammers often go after people who are new to a country, people who are learning English, and older people. Why? What can we do to protect them?

  • The warning sign I see most is …
  • AI makes scams harder to spot because …
  • Scammers go after … because …
  • If I get a message like this, I will …

🚀 Go Further

Make a safety card for your family, in your home language. Work with a partner.

  1. On your sheet, write the plan in English: the warning signs, “Never pay with gift cards,” “Never share a code,” “Call back on a number you know,” and who to ask for help.
  2. Ask your class AI tool to translate it into your home language. Do not type any names, phone numbers, or your family’s safe word.
  3. Check the translation. You’re the expert (Lesson 3.5). Fix any mistakes.
  4. Add your family’s phone numbers by hand, at home, on the paper card.

No class AI tool today? Translate it yourself, or with a family member.

At home, with your family, look at the text messages and emails you got this week. Did any have warning signs? Don’t click any links. Tell the class what you found, without names or numbers.
