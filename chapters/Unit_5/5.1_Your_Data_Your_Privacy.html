5.1 - Your Data, Your Privacy

In 2018, reporters at The New York Times looked at location data that phone apps collected and sold. The data had no names in it. But the dots on the map showed one person going from the same house every morning to the same school every day.

The reporters found her. She was a math teacher. The apps knew where she lived, where she worked, and where she went after work.

Your phone collects information about you all day. Some of it’s even hidden inside your photos. Today you find it.

Today’s Plan (40 minutes)

  • 🔥 Warm Up What does your phone know? 6 min
  • 👥 Learn Together Data you give and data you leak 10 min
  • 🛠️ Try It Find the hidden location in a photo 17 min
  • 💬 Talk About It Is a free app really free? 7 min
  • 🔒 privacy: your right to keep information about yourself to yourself
  • 🏷️ metadata: hidden information about a file, like when and where a photo was taken
  • 📍 location data: information that shows where your phone has been
  • 🗂️ data: information a computer can store and use

Printable lesson sheet - the sheet you write on today.

🔥 Warm Up

Work with a partner. List everything you think your phone knows about you. Then share with the class.

Where you go. Who you call and text. Your photos. What you search for. What videos you watch, and how long. When you sleep. Your voice, if you talk to it. Your face, if it unlocks with your face.

👥 Learn Together

5.1.1 - Data You Give and Data You Leak

Data you give Data you leak
You know you’re sharing it. You may not know you’re sharing it.
What you type, post, and upload Where your phone is
Your name when you sign up What you click, and how long you look
A photo you post Hidden information inside the photo

Companies use this data to sell ads and to choose what to show you next. AI makes this easier. It can look at millions of small pieces of data and make guesses about you. Where do you live? What do you like? What might you buy?

5.1.2 - Photos Have Hidden Information

When a phone takes a photo, it often saves hidden information inside the file. This is called metadata. It can include:

  • the kind of phone or camera
  • the date and time
  • the exact place where the photo was taken

If you send someone the photo file, they may get the place too. A photo of your dog in your backyard can show your home address.

Many social media apps remove the location from a photo when you post it. Sending the photo file in a text or an email may keep it. Most phones let you turn off location for the camera, in the phone’s privacy settings.

🛠️ Try It

5.1.3 - Find the Hidden Location

Work with a partner. This demo runs on your computer. The photo never leaves it.

  1. Press Use the sample photo. What hidden information is inside it? Write it on your sheet.
  2. Press Open this place on a map. Where was the photo taken?
  3. Press Make a clean copy. Is the location still there?
  4. Talk with your partner: who could find you from a photo you sent?
Choose a photo to see what is hidden inside it.
⬇️ Save the clean copy

Don’t put photos of yourself or anyone else into your class AI tool or any website. If you try the demo with your own photo, it stays on this computer. Don’t show a classmate’s location to anyone.

💬 Talk About It

Many apps are free. The company makes money from your data. Is that a fair trade? Should apps be allowed to sell where you go?

  • My phone knows … about me.
  • The sample photo showed …
  • A free app is (fair / not fair) because …
  • Apps (should / shouldn’t) be allowed to sell location data, because …

🚀 Go Further

Apps ask for permission to use your location, camera, microphone, and contacts. Work with a partner.

  1. On your sheet, look at the 6 apps and the permissions they ask for.
  2. For each one, decide: does the app need it to work? Write yes or no.
  3. Choose one permission you would turn off. Why?
  4. Try it at home: open your phone’s settings. Find Privacy or Permissions. Which apps can see your location? Turn off one you don’t need.

At home, show someone in your family how to turn off location for the camera on their phone. Explain why, in your home language.
