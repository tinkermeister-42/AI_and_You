3.8 - Project: Solve a Real Problem

Your mom gets a letter from the school, in English, with a deadline. You want a job at a grocery store, and you need a résumé. You have a biology test next week. You want to sell the bracelets you make.

These are real problems. In this unit, you learned to ask good questions, keep talking, get feedback, check facts, test translations, check math, and make pictures. Now put it all together. Use AI to help solve a problem in your life.

Today’s Plan (40 minutes)

  • 🔥 Warm Up What is a problem in your life? 5 min
  • 👥 Learn Together What makes a good project 8 min
  • 🛠️ Try It Plan it and start it 22 min
  • 💬 Talk About It What could go wrong? 5 min
  • 📦 product: the thing you make in a project: a flyer, a guide, a plan
  • 📒 prompt log: a list of every prompt you used, and what you did with the answer
  • 📊 rubric: a chart that shows how your work will be graded
  • ✔️ fact-check: to find out if something is true, using good sources

Printable lesson sheet - your project plan.

🔥 Warm Up

Write 3 problems in your life or your family’s life. Big or small. Then circle the one you care about most.

Reading letters in English. Finding a job. Studying for a test. Planning a meal with what is in the kitchen. Saving money for something. Learning to drive. Planning a birthday party. Helping a younger brother or sister with homework.

👥 Learn Together

3.8.1 - Choose a Good Problem

A good project problem is:

  • Real: you or your family really have this problem.
  • Small: you can finish it in about 2 class days.
  • Useful: you make something you’ll really use.
Problem Your product Unit 3 skills
My family gets school letters in English. A guide to school letters, in your language prompts, translation check
I want a job. A résumé and 5 practice interview answers feedback, follow-up questions
I have a test next week. A study guide and a practice quiz prompts, fact-checking
I want to sell what I make. The words for a flyer, and a price plan prompts, feedback, math check
I want to cook for my family. A week of meals and a shopping list prompts, math check

3.8.2 - The Plan

Your plan answers 6 questions. You write it on your sheet.

  1. What is the problem?
  2. Who has the problem?
  3. What will you make? (your product)
  4. How will AI help?
  5. What will you check, and how?
  6. What will you do yourself?

3.8.3 - The Rules

  1. Use at least 3 skills from Unit 3.
  2. Keep a prompt log: every prompt you use, and what you did with the answer.
  3. Check what the AI tells you. Write how you checked.
  4. Write an AI note at the end. It’s short and honest. It says what you did and what the AI did (Lesson 3.3).
  5. No private information in the AI tool: no names, pictures of people, addresses, phone numbers, birthdays, or student numbers.
Got it Almost Not yet
Real problem The product will really help someone. It could help, but it isn’t finished. It isn’t a real problem.
Prompts The log shows good prompts and follow-ups. The log has some prompts. No log.
Checking I checked the facts, math, or translation, and fixed mistakes. I checked some things. I didn’t check.
Honesty My AI note says what the AI did and what I did. My AI note is short. No AI note.

This chart is the rubric. It shows how your project is graded.

🛠️ Try It

3.8.4 - Plan It and Start It

Work with a partner, or in a group of 3. (In a class of 4, make two pairs.) Choose a problem you both care about.

  1. Fill in the plan on your sheet.
  2. Show your plan to your teacher. Your teacher signs it.
  3. Write your first prompt. Use task, context, and format (Lesson 3.1).
  4. Write the prompt in your prompt log. Write what you did with the answer.

For a résumé, use a fake name and address in the AI tool, like “Student Name, 123 Main Street.” Put your real information in only at the very end, on your own computer, never in the AI tool. Before you type a school letter into the AI tool, take out every name and number.

💬 Talk About It

What could go wrong if the AI makes a mistake in your project? Who could it hurt? How will you catch it?

  • Our problem is … , and our product is …
  • If the AI makes a mistake, … could happen.
  • We’ll check … by …
  • We’ll do … ourselves, because …

🚀 Go Further

Use the second day to finish your product.

  1. Read your prompt log. What is left to do?
  2. Keep going: follow-up questions, checks, and changes. Log each prompt.
  3. Finish the product.
  4. Check everything once more: facts, math, translation.
  5. Write your AI note.
  6. Bring your product, plan, and prompt log to Lesson 3.9.
