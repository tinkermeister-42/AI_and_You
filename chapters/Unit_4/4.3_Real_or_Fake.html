4.3 - Real or Fake?

In March 2023, a photo of Pope Francis went around the world. He was wearing a huge, white, puffy winter jacket, like a rapper. Millions of people shared it. Many believed it.

It wasn’t a photo. A man in Chicago made it with an AI image generator, as a joke. He didn’t expect so many people to believe it.

Today you test your eyes against AI pictures. Then you learn something surprising: looking for clues stops working. You need a better way to check.

Today’s Plan (40 minutes)

  • 🔥 Warm Up Which picture is AI? 7 min
  • 👥 Learn Together Why the clues stop working 10 min
  • 🛠️ Try It Trace it back 17 min
  • 💬 Talk About It What happens to trust? 6 min
  • 🎭 deepfake: a fake picture, video, or voice of a real person, made with AI
  • 🔄 reverse image search: searching with a picture to find where else it’s online
  • 🗣️ voice clone: an AI copy of a person’s voice, made from a short recording
  • 📚 source: where a fact comes from: a book, a website, a person

Printable lesson sheet - the sheet you write on today.

🔥 Warm Up

Can you spot a picture made by AI? Before you start, guess your score. Write your guess on your sheet.

Work with a partner. Open Which Image Is AI? Each round shows two pictures. One is a real photo. One was made by AI. Click the one you think is AI. Then press Next.

Keep going. When you see End & see stats, press it. Write your score on your sheet. Then the class finds its average score. Were you as good as you guessed?

The site keeps your answers, with no name, to compare you with other players. Don’t sign up, and don’t share your score.

If you guessed with a coin, you’d get about 50% right. Look at your end screen. How did you do, compared with other people?

In a 2026 study, scientists in Australia tested people on real faces and AI faces. Most people were sure they could spot the AI faces. But they did only a little better than guessing. Even people with an amazing memory for faces did only a little better. Feeling sure isn’t the same as being right.

👥 Learn Together

4.3.1 - Why the Clues Stop Working

An AI fake of a real person, like the Pope picture, is called a deepfake. People used to look for clues like these:

Old clue What happened
Hands with 6 fingers Newer tools draw hands well.
Messy words on signs Newer tools can write words.
Earrings that don’t match Newer tools fix this.
Strange, blurry backgrounds Newer tools fix this too.

Every time people find a clue, the companies fix it. So looking for clues in the picture doesn’t work for long.

4.3.2 - Check the Source, Not the Picture

Fact-checkers don’t stare at a picture. They ask where it came from. Use the four steps called SIFT:

Step What you do
Stop Before you share it, stop. Do you feel very angry or surprised? Be careful.
Investigate the source Who posted it first? Are they a real news organization?
Find better coverage Search for it. Are trusted news sources reporting it?
Trace to the original Use a reverse image search to find the first place it appeared.

4.3.3 - Fake Voices

Fakes aren’t only pictures. AI can make fake voices, too. It can copy a voice from a short recording, maybe a video someone posted online. Scammers use voice clone calls that sound like a family member in trouble: “Mom, I need money right now!”

Choose a family safe word: a secret word that only your family knows. If someone calls in a panic asking for money, ask for the safe word. Then hang up and call the person back on their real number. Calling back is the same idea as SIFT: you check the source, not the voice.

🛠️ Try It

4.3.4 - Trace It Back

Work with a partner. Your teacher shows 2 pictures that went around online. For each one:

  1. Stop. Write how the picture makes you feel.
  2. Find better coverage. Search for a few words that describe it, plus “fact check.” Or describe the picture to your class AI tool and ask: “Have fact-checkers written about this picture? Give me sources.” Then open the sources yourself.
  3. Trace to the original. Your teacher does a reverse image search on the projector. Where did the picture first appear? Who posted it?
  4. Decide: real, fake, or real but used in a misleading way. Write your proof.

Never make or share a fake picture, video, or voice of a real person, even as a joke. Making or sharing fake sexual pictures of a real person is a crime in the United States. If it happens to you or someone you know, tell a trusted adult right away.

💬 Talk About It

If anyone can make a fake video of anyone, what happens to trust? A politician caught on a real video could just say, “That is AI.” How do we know what is real?

  • On the AI picture test, I guessed …% and got …%. It was (easy / hard) because …
  • Looking for clues stops working because …
  • Before I share a picture, I will …
  • When anyone can make fakes, people might …

🚀 Go Further

Make a poster of the SIFT steps in your home language. Add one real example of a fake picture that went around online, and how people found out.

Explain voice clones to your family, in your home language. Help them choose a safe word. (Don’t tell anyone at school what it is!)
