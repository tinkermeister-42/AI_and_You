Syllabus

AI and You A mini-block class with Mr. Ross, Frederick Douglass High School

About the Class

AI is everywhere now. It writes, draws, talks, answers questions, and even pretends to be a friend. Some people say it thinks like a human brain. Is that true?

In this class, you’ll learn how AI was built and how it works. You’ll use it for real tasks. You’ll act out famous experiments, train a neural network made of people, and test AI in your own language.

By the end, you’ll know how to use AI well and how to catch it when it’s wrong. And you’ll decide for yourself what you think about it, and about the world it’s building.

About Your Teacher

Before teaching, Mr. Ross spent more than 15 years working in AI and data science. He worked at Wolfram Research, the makers of Mathematica. Then he spent nearly ten years helping build a data science team at a Missouri company. He has a background in biology and statistics, and he still does AI work outside of school. He has worked with these tools since long before ChatGPT existed.

What You Will Learn

  • How AI was built, from the first ideas in the 1950s to the chatbots of today
  • How neural networks learn, and the idea they copied from brain cells
  • How to use AI well for writing, research, translation, math, and pictures
  • How to catch AI when it makes things up, is unfair, or is used to fake things
  • How AI is changing privacy, jobs, the news, creativity, and friendship
  • What AI might mean for the future, and what you can do about it

The Big Question

On the first day, you answer one question: Is AI like a human brain? Your teacher keeps your answer. On the last day, you answer again, with everything you learned in between.

Unit 1: Where AI Came From

The story of AI, from Alan Turing’s question “Can machines think?” to ChatGPT. You play the imitation game and act out the Klingon Room. You play against a game bot made of rules. You label pictures the way the ImageNet workers did. The class builds a timeline on the wall. You also write your first answer to the Big Question.

Unit 2: Brains and Neural Networks

How simple rules make complex behavior, how brain cells work, and how computer scientists copied the idea. You become a neuron, then the class trains a neural network made of people, using cards for made-up creatures. You build and train a real AI on a computer. You train your own AI with a camera. You see how AI sees pictures and how chatbots write.

Unit 3: Using AI Well

The heart of the class. You learn to write good prompts and ask follow-up questions. You get feedback on your own writing. You check facts, test translations in your home language, check AI’s math, and write prompts for pictures. Then you finish a real project that uses AI to solve a problem in your life. You share it with the class.

Unit 4: When AI Gets It Wrong

AI makes things up. It can be unfair to some people. It can be used to fake pictures and voices. You catch a chatbot inventing facts about our school. You act out the story of Clever Hans the horse. You test your eyes against fake faces. And you find out why AI cheat detectors blame students who are learning English.

Unit 5: AI in the Real World

What AI means for your privacy, your future job, and the news you see. You find the hidden location inside a photo. You sort real messages from scams. You break your dream job into tasks. And you watch a news feed learn what you like.

Unit 6: AI and Our Future

AI art, AI friends, the hidden costs of AI, the rules for AI, and possible futures. You act out the trolley problem and the paper clip machine. In the last lesson, you replace a “brain” one cell at a time. Then you read your answer from the first day, and answer the Big Question again.

How Each Class Works

Every lesson has a hands-on activity.

  • Warm Up: a quick question or game.
  • Learn Together: one big idea, with pictures and examples.
  • Try It: a demo on the page, our class AI tool, or an activity you act out.
  • Talk About It: a hard question to talk about with a partner. Sentence starters help.
  • Go Further: more to do in class on a second day, when there’s time.
  • Explore More: extra pages to read on your own, if you want to learn more.

Each lesson has a printable sheet to write on. If you miss a class, read the lesson page and do the Try It. The page has everything you need.

What We Use

  • A Chromebook and this book. The demos in the book work in any web browser, need no account, and send nothing anywhere.
  • Our class AI tool: Raina, in MagicSchool. This is the only AI tool you use with an account in this class.
  • A few free websites that need no login, like Google Translate and Teachable Machine. Your teacher checks each one first.

Your Privacy

Never type or upload personal information into any AI tool. That means no names, yours or anyone else’s. No pictures of yourself or other people. No addresses, phone numbers, birthdays, or student numbers. When a prompt needs a person, write “my friend” or “my grandmother” instead.

Using AI Honestly

In this class, you learn when AI helps you learn and when it does the learning for you. Each assignment says how much AI you may use. If it doesn’t say, ask first. When you use AI, write a short AI note that says what it did and what you did.
