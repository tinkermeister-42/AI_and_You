{\large\textbf{2. How ELIZA works.}}

\begin{center}\resizebox{0.7\linewidth}{!}{\fig{Unit_1/1.3_eliza_steps}}\end{center}

Now you are ELIZA. Your partner types: \textbf{``I am worried about my test.''}

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{tabular}{@{}R{1.9in}l}
1. Find the key words: & \ansline{4.0in}{I am} \\[0.8em]
2. Copy the rest: & \ansline{4.0in}{worried about my test} \\[0.8em]
3. Swap words: & \ansline{4.0in}{worried about your test \ (my $\to$ your)} \\[0.8em]
4. Fill in the reply: & \ansline{4.0in}{How long have you been worried about your test?} \\
\end{tabular}

{\large\textbf{3. People believed it.}}

\vspace{0.4em}
When people feel that a computer understands them, but it does not, we call it the \ansline{1.6in}{ELIZA effect}.

\vspace{0.9em}
Weizenbaum said some jobs should not be given to computers, even if a computer \ansline{1.6in}{can do them}.

\vspace{1.3em}

{\large\textbf{5. The Chinese Room.}} Work in a group of 2 to 4.

\vspace{0.5em}
Did the person in the room understand Chinese? \ansline{1.2in}{no}

\vspace{0.9em}
How do you know? \ansline{5.4in}{They only matched shapes in the rule book.}

\vspace{0.9em}
Note 6 asked: ``Can you speak Chinese?'' The room said: \ansline{2.9in}{Yes, I can speak Chinese.}

\vspace{0.9em}
Was that true? \ansline{0.8in}{no} \quad Did the replies make sense to the people outside? \ansline{1.4in}{yes}
