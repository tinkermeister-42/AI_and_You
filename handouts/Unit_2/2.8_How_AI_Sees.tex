{\large\textbf{2. Pixels are numbers.}}

\vspace{0.4em}

{\large\textbf{3. A filter is a neuron.}}

\vspace{0.4em}
A filter is a \ansline{0.9in}{neuron} with 9 inputs. Its 9 numbers are its \ansline{0.9in}{weights}.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
0 & 0 & 255 \\ \hline
0 & 0 & 255 \\ \hline
0 & 0 & 255 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\ $\times$\ 
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
$-1$ & 0 & $+1$ \\ \hline
$-1$ & 0 & $+1$ \\ \hline
$-1$ & 0 & $+1$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\qquad Total $=$ \ansline{0.8in}{765} \quad Does it fire? \ansline{0.5in}{yes}

\vspace{0.6em}
If all 9 picture numbers are 255, the total is \ansline{0.5in}{0}. Is there an edge? \ansline{0.5in}{no}

{\large\textbf{4. Layers.}}

\vspace{0.4em}
Layer 1 finds \ansline{0.9in}{edges}. Layer 2 looks at layer 1's maps, not the picture. It finds \ansline{0.9in}{corners}.

\vspace{0.6em}
In the pictures from a real network, early layers find \ansline{0.9in}{edges}. Later layers find parts and whole \ansline{0.9in}{objects}.

\vspace{0.6em}
Who chose the real network's filters? \ansline{3.6in}{Nobody. Training found them.}
