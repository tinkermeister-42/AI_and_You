{\large\textbf{2. The parts of a neuron.}}

\begin{center}\resizebox{0.82\linewidth}{!}{\fig{Unit_2/2.2_neuron_blank}}\end{center}

\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\ }l@{\qquad}l@{\ }l}
\textbf{1} = & \ansline{1.6in}{dendrites} & \textbf{2} = & \ansline{1.6in}{cell body} \\[0.9em]
\textbf{3} = & \ansline{1.6in}{axon} & \textbf{4} = & \ansline{1.6in}{synapse} \\
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.9em}
The \ansline{1.2in}{dendrites} take signals in. The \ansline{1.2in}{cell body} adds them up.

\vspace{0.8em}
If the total is big enough, the neuron \ansline{0.9in}{fires}. The \ansline{0.9in}{axon} carries the signal away.

\vspace{0.8em}
At the \ansline{1.0in}{synapse}, chemicals carry the signal to the next neuron.

\vspace{0.8em}
Your brain has about \ansline{1.2in}{86 billion} neurons.
