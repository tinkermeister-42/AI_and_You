Course Map

Every lesson is one class (40 minutes). Every lesson has something to try with your hands.

Most lessons also have a 🚀 Go Further part. It can fill a second day, or you can do it on your own.

Unit 1: Where AI Came From

Lesson What you do
1.1 What Is AI? Sort things into AI and not AI. Write a rule for “cat.” Play Quick, Draw!
1.2 Can a Machine Think? (1950) Play Turing’s imitation game. Catch the AI hiding among your classmates.
1.3 The First Chatbot (1966) Be ELIZA with a rule card. Act out the Chinese Room, in Klingon.
1.4 Rules Win at Games (1956 to 1997) Play a tic-tac-toe bot. Turn its rules off one at a time until you can beat it.
1.5 Data Changes Everything (2009 to 2016) Label pictures like the ImageNet workers did. Find where people disagree.
1.6 Transformers and ChatGPT (2017 to today) Find what “it” means in a sentence. Build a sentence one vote at a time, like a chatbot.

Unit 2: Brains and Neural Networks

Lesson What you do
2.1 Simple Rules, Complex Behavior Make a few rows of Rule 30 by hand, then explore hundreds of rules on screen and sort what they make. Watch the Game of Life.
2.2 Your Brain Is Made of Cells Measure your reaction time with a falling ruler. Send a signal around the room.
2.3 How Brains Learn Practice a hard task (writing with your other hand). Graph how you get better. Meet Pavlov’s dogs.
2.4 The Artificial Neuron One neuron sorts made-up creatures into Fighters and Wizards. The class fixes its weights by hand, then sees how neurons connect in layers.
2.5 Train a Human Neural Network One neuron can’t learn the club rule, so the class becomes a network with a hidden layer. Feed it creature cards and play Who’s to Blame after every mistake.
2.6 Build a Real AI Train a real neural network on 300 creatures, and watch its weights change while it learns. Then test it, and find what it can’t learn.
2.7 Train Your Own AI Teach Teachable Machine your own hand signs. Then try to fool it.
2.8 How AI Sees A picture is numbers. A filter is a neuron that finds edges. Watch layers go from edges to corners, and see what real networks find layer by layer.
2.9 How Chatbots Work Build a tiny chatbot that counts which word comes next and turns counts into chances. Then see a real chatbot as a network with one neuron for every word.

Unit 3: Using AI Well

Lesson What you do
3.1 Asking Good Questions Make weak prompts better. Compare the answers side by side.
3.2 Keep Talking Fix a bad answer with follow-up questions.
3.3 Writing with AI, Not by AI Get feedback on your own paragraph. Decide which advice to keep.
3.4 Research and Checking Ask AI a research question. Check every fact against a real source.
3.5 AI and Your Language Translate there and back again. You’re the expert who grades the AI.
3.6 AI and Math Catch a wrong step. Test a chatbot’s math with a calculator.
3.7 Making Pictures with AI Watch noise turn into a picture, step by step. Who gets credit for AI art?
3.8 Project: Solve a Real Problem Pick a problem in your life. Plan how AI could help.
3.9 Project: Feedback and Share Gallery walk: give and get feedback. Choose what to change.

Unit 4: When AI Gets It Wrong

Lesson What you do
4.1 AI Makes Things Up Watch a tiny chatbot mix true facts into false ones. Ask AI about our school and town, and catch what it invents.
4.2 Where Bias Comes From Act out Clever Hans, the horse that seemed to do math. Meet the AI that learned snow instead of wolves.
4.3 Real or Fake? Guess which pictures are AI. Learn why the clues stop working, and what to check instead.
4.4 Why AI Cheat Detectors Fail Watch a detector call human writing “AI.” Find out who it blames most.

Unit 5: AI in the Real World

Lesson What you do
5.1 Your Data, Your Privacy Check what your apps can see. Find the hidden location in a photo.
5.2 AI Scams Sort real messages from scams. Make a family safety plan in your home language.
5.3 AI and Jobs Break your dream job into tasks. Sort what AI can do, can help with, and can’t do. Will robots do the trades too?
5.4 AI and the News Compare two news feeds. Trace a fake picture back to where it started.

Unit 6: AI and Our Future

Lesson What you do
6.1 Can AI Be Creative? Vote in a blind poem show: person or AI? Then decide who owns AI art.
6.2 AI Friends Sort what a friend does and what an app is built to do.
6.3 The Hidden Costs Estimate the energy and water behind AI. Meet the people who label data.
6.4 Who Makes the Rules? Act out the trolley problem, then decide what an AI car should do. Who should make the rules?
6.5 Possible Futures Act out the paper clip machine. Find out what experts hope and fear. Decide what you can do.
6.6 The Big Question, Again Replace a brain one cell at a time: is it still you? Read your answer from the first day. Answer again with what you know now.
