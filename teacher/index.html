How the Lessons Fit the Calendar

  • 38 lessons, about 64 meetings. Each lesson has a 40-minute core. The core is planned at twice the usual time, for language support. Go Further fills a second day when there is time. When you are behind, skip Go Further; never squeeze a core.
  • Explore More pages are for students working on their own. They are never needed for a core.
  • Raina (MagicSchool) is the only AI tool students use with an account. Every Raina activity has a fallback: you run it on the projector, or an unplugged version. Images share a usage limit, so plan pairs or turns.
  • No personal information goes into any AI tool: no names, pictures of people, addresses, phone numbers, birthdays, or student numbers.
  • Blocked for students (October 2026): youtube.com, youtube-nocookie.com, ytimg.com, lens.google.com, tineye.com. Each video has an “At school” link to yout-ube.com, which is not blocked. If that stops working, show videos from your account on the projector. Run reverse image searches (Lesson 4.3) on the projector.
  • Answer keys sit beside each student sheet: handouts/Unit_X/<name>_KEY.pdf. Nothing links to them.

Unit 2: Brains and Neural Networks

2.1 - Simple Rules, Complex Behavior

Sheets: Simple Rules Complex Behavior (key)

Class size. The animation is the wave everyone sees, so the warm-up works with any class size. With 10 or more students, also make a real wave down a row. With 4 or 5, pass a small one around a table.

2.2 - Your Brain Is Made of Cells

Sheets: Your Brain Is Made of Cells (key)

Small class?. With only 4 or 5 people, the squeeze goes around too fast to time. Go around the circle 5 times without stopping. Then divide the time by 5.

2.3 - How Brains Learn

Sheets: How Brains Learn (key)

2.4 - The Artificial Neuron

Sheets: Creature Cards (key) · The Artificial Neuron (key)

Before class. Print and cut one set of creature cards. Fold each card so the type is hidden.

The creatures are made up, from a recipe in scripts/make-creatures.py: Fighters have high Strength and low Magic, Wizards the opposite, with some overlap. The same 300 creatures are used in 2.4, 2.5, and 2.6, so the class can compare what each network learns with the real recipe (Explore More, Unit 2).

Class size and hints. 4 students: each student takes one or two cards. More students: groups of 2 or 3, one card each. One student changes the numbers in the tool on the screen. Extra students check each group’s math. Big class: two teams fix their own neuron. Who gets 6 of 6 first?

If the class is stuck: “Lullom has more Magic. Which weight should push its total down?” One answer that works: Strength weight 1, Magic weight −1, threshold 0. Many others work too. The starting numbers get 2 of 6 right.

2.5 - Train a Human Neural Network

Sheets: Creature Cards (key) · Train a Human Neural Network (key)

Before class. Print and cut one set of creature cards for each network. Fold each card so the answer is hidden.

Put this page on the screen. The tool in 2.5.3 is the class board: it draws the network with every number, and its highlighted numbers show each fix. Press Next step only after the class has worked out each step by hand. The key on the sheet follows the cards in order: the network makes 5 mistakes in pass 1, and 0 in pass 2.

Class size. 4 students: one student for each neuron, and one dealer who is also the checker and scorekeeper. 5 to 9: two or three students on each neuron team. 10 or more: make two networks with two sets of cards. Both start with the same numbers. Which network learns first? Do they end with the same numbers? (The tool on the screen follows one network. The other network checks against it.)

Timing: a card takes about a minute, a fix about two. The key path makes 5 fixes in pass 1 (T1, T2, T3, T5, T6) and none in pass 2.

2.6 - Build a Real AI

Sheets: Build a Real AI (key)

Before class. Open the page on the projector. Do Run 1 together at slow speed, so everyone sees the lines change and learns to read the picture. Then pairs do the other runs on Chromebooks. Each run takes a few seconds at normal speed, and about 30 seconds at slow speed.

Scores change a little with each new random split. The patterns in the think boxes stay the same.

2.7 - Train Your Own AI

Sheets: Train Your Own AI (key)

Class size and cameras. Small class: each pair trains its own model, then you swap computers and test each other’s. Large class, or no cameras? The teacher trains one model on the projector. Everyone comes up and adds pictures of their hands. A class model has more kinds of hands in it. Is it better?

Unit 3: Using AI Well

3.1 - Asking Good Questions

Sheets: Asking Good Questions (key)

Big class? Make it a race. Split into two teams. The teacher puts one weak prompt on the projector. Each team writes a better prompt in 3 minutes. The teacher tries both on the projector. The class votes: which answer is more useful? Then switch to a new weak prompt.

3.2 - Keep Talking

Sheets: Keep Talking (key)

Class size. Small class (4 or 5): work as one group. Each person chooses the next follow-up move. Big class: two teams get the same topic and 3 follow-ups each. Read both final answers aloud. Which team got the more useful answer?

3.3 - Writing with AI, Not by AI

Sheets: Writing with AI (key)

3.4 - Research and Checking

Sheets: Research and Checking (key)

Class size. Small class (4 or 5): work as one group. Each person checks a different fact. Big class: make it a race between teams. A team gets 1 point for each fact checked in 2 sources. A team loses 2 points for a check that’s wrong. Speed is good. Being right is better.

3.5 - AI and Your Language

Sheets: AI and Your Language (key)

Class size. Small class: each student tests their own language. Then everyone looks at the class chart together. Big class: students who share a language make a team. Each team makes a poster: the AI’s best and worst translation.

3.6 - AI and Math

Sheets: AI and Math (key)

Class size. Small class: everyone tests the chatbot, then the class makes one chart together. Big class: each pair tests only 2 of the 4 problems, but asks each one 3 times in new chats. Does the AI give the same answer every time?

3.7 - Making Pictures with AI

Sheets: Making Pictures with AI (key)

Before class. Picture tools share one usage limit with graphs. Plan turns for pairs, and be ready to make the pictures on the projector.

3.8 - Project: Solve a Real Problem

Sheets: Project Solve a Real Problem (key)

3.9 - Project: Feedback and Share

Sheets: Project Feedback and Share (key)

Class size. Small class (4 or 5): sit in a circle. Pass your project to the right. Write a note. Keep passing until everyone has read every project. Big class: 3 rounds of 4 minutes. The teacher says when to move.

Unit 4: When AI Gets It Wrong

4.1 - AI Makes Things Up

Sheets: AI Makes Things Up (key)

Before class. Have the real answers ready for the questions about our school and Columbia: the year the school opened, the principal, the mascot, about how many students, and the mayor.

Class size. Small class: work as one group and test all the questions together. Big class: make it a contest. Each team gets 1 point for every made-up fact it catches and proves wrong with a real source.

4.2 - Where Bias Comes From

Sheets: Where Bias Comes From (key)

Before class. For Clever Hans, Round 2: seal a math problem in an envelope. Nobody in the room should know the answer.

Class size. 4 students: Hans, a questioner, and a crowd of 2. Big class: make two Hans teams. Each team tries to fool the other team’s crowd in Round 1.

4.3 - Real or Fake?

Sheets: Real or Fake (key)

Before class. Choose 2 pictures that spread online recently for Trace It Back. Reverse image search (TinEye, Google Lens) is blocked for students: run it from your account on the projector, or have a fact-check article ready for each picture.

For the Warm Up, check that sightengine.com loads on a student Chromebook. Its pictures come from s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com. If either is blocked, play it on the projector and let the class vote on each round.

Class size. Small class: work together on both pictures, then try the Pope picture from the top of the page. Big class: each pair gets one picture. Pairs with the same picture compare their proof. Then each group teaches the class.

4.4 - Why AI Cheat Detectors Fail

Sheets: Why AI Cheat Detectors Fail (key)

Class size. Small class: everyone tests their own paragraph, then the class looks at all the results together. Big class: make a big chart on the board with two columns: “Human writing called AI” and “Human writing called human.” Every pair adds tally marks.

Unit 5: AI in the Real World

5.1 - Your Data, Your Privacy

Sheets: Your Data Your Privacy (key)

5.2 - AI Scams

Sheets: AI Scams (key)

Class size. Small class: go through the messages together on the projector. Everyone votes before each answer. Big class: two teams. Each team gets a point for each right answer and a point for naming a warning sign the other team missed.

5.3 - AI and Jobs

Sheets: AI and Jobs (key)

Class size. Small class: everyone sorts one job together first (the nurse), then pairs choose their own. Big class: pairs with the same kind of job (health, building, business, computers) join into a group and compare their sorts.

5.4 - AI and the News

Sheets: AI and the News (key)

Before class. For Day 2, choose one news story or picture that spread online this month.

Class size. Small class: one student trains the feed on the projector while the class tells them what to click. Then a second student tries. Big class: every pair compares, then the class counts: how many pairs had feeds that were almost the same?

Unit 6: AI and Our Future

6.1 - Can AI Be Creative?

Sheets: Can AI Be Creative (key)

Before class. For Day 2, make AI pictures of the same things students draw, or plan turns if pairs make them.

Class size. Small class: read the poems aloud together, one at a time, and vote after each. Big class: two teams. Each team must agree on one vote per poem, and explain its best clue.

6.2 - AI Friends

Sheets: AI Friends (key)

Class size. Small class: do the sort together on the board. Big class: pairs sort first. Then two pairs join and compare. Talk about every card you put in different circles.

6.3 - The Hidden Costs

Sheets: The Hidden Costs (key)

Class size. Small class: do the math together, one step at a time, with the calculator on the screen. Big class: each pair guesses a different number of questions per day (5, 10, 20, 50). Compare the results: how much does the guess change the answer?

6.4 - Who Makes the Rules?

Sheets: Who Makes the Rules (key)

Before class. Bring tape to make two tracks on the floor, and paper people for a small class.

Class size. 4 students: one at the lever, and paper people on the tracks. Everyone votes. Big class: take turns at the lever. Students on the tracks vote too.

6.5 - Possible Futures

Sheets: Possible Futures (key)

Before class. Bring paper strips and tape for the paper chains, and write the robot’s goal card.

Class size. 4 students: one robot, one loophole finder, and two rule writers. Big class: two teams. Each team writes rules for the other team’s robot. The team whose rules have the fewest loopholes wins.

6.6 - The Big Question, Again

Sheets: The Big Question Again (key)

Before class. Bring back the Big Question answers from Lesson 1.1. Make orange cards and rule cards for the chips.

Class size. 4 students: make a line of 4 with the teacher. Replace one student each round. Big class: make two lines. One line stays all neurons. Compare the squeeze times.

These “Right now (fall 2026)” boxes go out of date. Check each one before you teach that lesson.

  • 1.6 - Transformers and ChatGPT: There are many chatbots built this way: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Llama, and more.
  • 3.1 - Asking Good Questions: Your class AI tool is Raina, in MagicSchool.
  • 3.5 - AI and Your Language: About half of all websites are in English.
  • 3.6 - AI and Math: In July 2025, AI from Google DeepMind and OpenAI scored at gold-medal level at the International Mathematical Olympiad.
  • 4.2 - Where Bias Comes From: After people complained, Google Translate started showing both “he” and “she” for some short sentences in some languages.
  • 4.4 - Why AI Cheat Detectors Fail: Many schools still use AI detectors.
  • 5.1 - Your Data, Your Privacy: Many social media apps remove the location from a photo when you post it.
  • 5.2 - AI Scams: In 2025, people in the United States reported losing $15.9 billion to scams, more than ever before (Federal Trade Commission, March 2026).
  • 5.3 - AI and Jobs: Experts disagree about how fast AI is changing jobs.
  • 5.3 - AI and Jobs: Self-driving semi trucks carry freight on highways in Texas and nearby states.
  • 5.4 - AI and the News: NewsGuard is a company that checks news sites.
  • 6.1 - Can AI Be Creative?: In the United States, the Copyright Office says a work made only by AI can’t have a copyright.
  • 6.2 - AI Friends: Some families have taken companion app companies to court, saying the apps harmed their children.
  • 6.3 - The Hidden Costs: In 2025, OpenAI said people send ChatGPT about 2.5 billion questions every day.
  • 6.4 - Who Makes the Rules?: The European Union passed the first big AI law in 2024.
  • Explore More, Unit 3: Some AI tools now have a memory feature.
  • Explore More, Unit 3: Many chatbots can now search the web and show links.
  • Explore More, Unit 3: Image generators improved fast.
  • Explore More, Unit 4: Some cameras and AI tools now add Content Credentials: hidden information that says how a picture was made.
  • Explore More, Unit 5: AI companies keep changing their rules about chats.
